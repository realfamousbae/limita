Claude Code and Codex limits, one glance from the top of your Mac.

Limita is a tiny menu-bar app that shows how much of your 5-hour and weekly limits you have, without opening a terminal. It runs on Windows too, in the tray.

Download for macOS Download for Windows

Version 0.17.0 for macOS 14 or later and Windows 10 or 11. Free and open source.

Out of the way until you need it

No window to keep open and no Dock icon. Limita checks Claude every 3 minutes and Codex every 5, and shows the numbers where you already look.

  • 5h 15% used

    A pill at the top edge

    Rest the pointer at the top edge of any screen and a pill slides in with each service's 5-hour limit. Click it for the full dashboard. The notch and 80 points on each side of it are left to other apps.

  • ⌃control ⌥option L

    One shortcut from any app

    Press ⌃⌥L to open the dashboard at the top of the screen, even from a full-screen terminal. Press it again to close it. On Windows it is Ctrl+Alt+L.

  • ~/projects/app $ claude
    Claude 5h 52% · 7d 49% used │ Codex 5h 80% · 7d 64% left

    In iTerm2's status bar

    An optional plugin shows your limits in the status bar of tabs where Claude Code or Codex is running, and hides the bar everywhere else. Click the limits to open the dashboard.

  • PEAK HOURS
    USAGE LIMITS RUN OUT FASTER
    • Claudeweekdays, 5–11 AM Pacific
    • Codexweekdays, 12:00–18:00 UTC

    Knows the peak hours

    During a service's peak hours its limits run out faster. The dashboard shows a burning gauge then, and names the service when only one of two is affected.

  • Where the tokens went

    Activity graphs tokens per day for each service: the last week, a month calendar, or up to half a year. Above it are your total tokens, favorite model, messages and peak hour.

  • LIMITA 0.17.0 IS OUTCLICK TO UPDATE

    Updates in one click

    When a new version is out, a green button appears in the dashboard. Click it and Limita downloads the update, checks its signature, installs it and reopens.

  • BANKED RESET · USE BY OCT 31

    Knows when limits reset

    When Anthropic or OpenAI resets limits for everyone, the Mac app says so in that service's column and in a notification. It also notices when your own weekly limit starts over early, and when Codex gives you a reset to redeem. Resets for everyone come from claude-resets.com and codex-reset.com.

Reads the numbers, nothing else

Limita asks each service for your limits the way its own tools do. Your logins stay where they are.

Claude Code

Limita reads Claude Code's login from the Keychain (macOS asks once) and sends it only to api.anthropic.com, the same request Claude Code's /usage makes. It never stores or renews the login. If that fails, the numbers come from Claude Code's status line.

Codex

Limita asks the Codex CLI itself, through codex app-server, so your login stays inside the CLI. If that fails, it reads the latest limits from the session files in ~/.codex.

  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics.
  • Prompts, transcripts, project paths and session IDs are ignored.
  • Besides those, Limita asks GitHub for updates, and the Mac app asks claude-resets.com and codex-reset.com about resets for connected services. None of these requests carry anything about you or your usage.

Install

macOS 14 or later

  1. Download the DMG, open it and drag Limita into Applications.
  2. The build is not notarized yet. For the first launch, right-click Limita in Applications and choose Open, or use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  3. On the first Claude refresh, macOS asks whether Limita may read Claude Code-credentials. Choose Always Allow.

Windows 10 or 11

  1. Download the installer.
  2. Run it. It installs for your user, with no administrator rights. The installer is not signed yet: on the SmartScreen warning choose More info → Run anyway.
  3. Limita waits in the tray. Rest the pointer at the middle of the top edge of the screen for the pill.
